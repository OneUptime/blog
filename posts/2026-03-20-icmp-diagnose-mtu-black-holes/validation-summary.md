## Issues Found
- The introduction defined black holes too narrowly as a firewall-blocking problem. I changed it to reflect RFC 1191/RFC 2923 behavior: the sender fails to receive ICMP Fragmentation Needed because the message may be filtered or never generated.
- Step 1 treated any failure of large DF-set packets as proof of a black hole. I corrected this to distinguish silent failure from explicit fragmentation-needed or message-too-long errors, which indicate PMTUD is functioning rather than black-holed.
- Step 2 overstated what the tools can prove and included an invalid Debian/Ubuntu package name (`pmtud`). I replaced that guidance with `traceroute` usage that narrows the failing hop or segment, which matches the documented behavior of `traceroute --mtu` and DF-set probes.
- Step 3 treated absence of captured ICMP Type 3/Code 4 as definitive proof of upstream filtering. I corrected that to the technically accurate statement that the message is likely filtered upstream or not generated by the failing router, and I normalized the `tcpdump` filter syntax.

## Review Notes
- The post is correctly scoped to IPv4. The equivalent IPv6 diagnostic uses ICMPv6 Packet Too Big rather than ICMP Type 3 Code 4.
- The `iptables` examples are technically valid, but systems using `nftables`, `firewalld`, or managed firewalls would need equivalent policy there instead of `iptables`.
- `traceroute --mtu` can narrow the failing segment, but it does not always identify the exact router because fragmentation-needed signals may be reported by the previous hop and some devices cache MTU information.
