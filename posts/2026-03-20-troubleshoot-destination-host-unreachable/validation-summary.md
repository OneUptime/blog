## Issues Found
- The post described local "Destination Host Unreachable" as only a missing route. I changed this to "no usable path" because Linux can also report local host-unreachable when same-subnet ARP/neighbor resolution fails.
- The post said the packet "never left your host" without qualification. I clarified that the IP packet was not forwarded beyond the host, while ARP may still have been attempted on the local link.
- The route-check example used `ip route show` and implied the missing prefix was the only diagnosis. I changed it to `ip route get 10.20.30.1`, which asks the kernel for the route it would use for that destination.
- The default-gateway ping command could expand to multiple gateways or an empty value. I changed it to extract the first `via` gateway from `ip route show default` and only run `ping` when a gateway was found.
- The ARP table guidance said that a missing ARP entry means the host is unreachable. I replaced `arp -n` with the current iproute2 `ip neigh show` command and clarified that `INCOMPLETE` or `FAILED` after a ping attempt indicates ARP/Layer 2 failure.
- The router-generated case listed destination ICMP blocking as a cause of host-unreachable. I corrected this to final-hop ARP/neighbor failure or an explicit ICMP unreachable generated by a firewall/router ACL, since silent filtering normally causes timeouts.
- The "Request Timed Out" comparison described host-unreachable as only a routing failure. I broadened it to routing, forwarding, or neighbor-resolution failure.

## Review Notes
The examples are Linux-specific. `traceroute`, `arping`, `dhclient`, and `iptables` may be optional packages or replaced by distribution-specific tooling, but the command forms shown are valid where those tools are installed. `iptables` remains valid, though many modern Linux systems use nftables or iptables-nft compatibility.
