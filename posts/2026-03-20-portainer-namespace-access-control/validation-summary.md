## Issues Found
- The team-management navigation was incorrect. The post said `Settings → Teams`, but current Portainer docs place teams under `User-related → Teams`. I corrected the navigation and the user-add flow.
- The namespace-access workflow was incorrect. The post said to open a namespace and use an access-control panel, but Portainer documents namespace access via `Namespaces` and `Manage access` on the namespace row. I updated the steps accordingly.
- The post described namespace access as if Portainer exposed per-namespace access levels such as `Read-only`, `Read/Write`, and `Admin`. Portainer documents namespace assignment separately from RBAC role assignment. I replaced the access-level explanation with the documented role model (`Read-Only User`, `Standard User`, `Namespace Operator`) and noted that cluster-wide roles like `Operator` cannot be assigned to a single namespace.
- The autogenerated RBAC YAML was inaccurate. The post claimed Portainer creates specific objects such as `portainer-rw` and `portainer-rw-backend-team`, but Portainer documents built-in mappings such as `portainer-basic`, `portainer-edit`, `portainer-view`, `portainer-operator`, and `portainer-helpdesk`. I replaced the invented YAML with a doc-backed mapping summary.
- The custom RBAC example was incomplete and partly misleading. It defined `Role` objects without a `RoleBinding`, and it granted unnecessary verbs on the `pods/log` subresource. I corrected the rules and added a binding example so the RBAC example actually grants permissions.
- The access-testing section incorrectly used a Kubernetes service account to represent a Portainer team. Portainer team membership is not tested that way. I changed the guidance to distinguish Portainer-side validation from generic Kubernetes RBAC validation and updated the `kubectl auth can-i` examples to use a real Kubernetes subject tied to the sample `RoleBinding`.
- The audit section referenced the invented Portainer role binding name. I updated it to audit the custom binding example and added Portainer's Effective access viewer as the correct Portainer-side audit path.
- The direct user-access section referred to a `Users` tab and mentioned service-account-level access. Current docs describe selecting users or teams from the namespace access screen; service accounts are Kubernetes subjects, not Portainer users. I corrected the workflow and examples.

## Review Notes
- Portainer's current documentation includes the `Namespace Operator` role for Kubernetes environments. Portainer release notes indicate this role was introduced in Portainer `2.29.1` on April 23, 2025, so readers using older Portainer releases may see a different role set.
- The Portainer "Kubernetes roles and bindings" reference currently lives under an older documentation path, but it remains the authoritative Portainer source for the built-in Kubernetes RBAC object names used in the corrected post.
